02   /   MY RCC RESEARCH

What is RCC?

These explanations come from my STEM fair research paper, using my own words with small grammar and spelling fixes.

01 / THE KIDNEY

What is RCC?

RCC is a cancer that starts in the kidney. It usually starts in the renal tubules that process filtered fluid and extra water to form urine. The cancer cells multiply uncontrollably in that area and can make it harder for the renal tubules to function properly.

People should learn about RCC because they could then notice if they ever have any of the symptoms, and they would know what to do. [1]

02 / HOW IT GROWS

How a tumor forms

A healthy kidney cell turns into a cancer cell when the healthy cell develops mutations in its DNA and starts dividing uncontrollably. Then, when there are enough mutated cells, they clump up and form a tumor. These cells can also start changing in size and can damage nearby healthy tissue.

Some of the risk factors for someone to get RCC are smoking, obesity, and high blood pressure. RCC is also more common in men and older adults. [2]

03 / SUBTYPES

Three main types

The three main types of RCC are clear cell RCC, papillary RCC, and chromophobe RCC. They all differ in appearance. Clear cell RCC cells appear almost clear under a microscope, and papillary RCC has finger-like structures. Chromophobe RCC cells look different under a microscope and often grow more slowly than the other two. [2]

04 / SIGNS

Symptoms

When you have RCC, you may not realize it because the tumor starts off small and may not cause any noticeable symptoms. Once the tumor starts growing, symptoms can appear, such as blood in your urine, persistent side or lower back pain, and sometimes swollen veins around a testicle. [3]

05 / SPREAD

Metastasis

Once RCC starts growing uncontrollably, it can spread to other parts of the body when cancerous cells get into your bloodstream and travel to organs such as the lungs. When the cancer cells reach your lungs, they can form new tumors that may interfere with breathing. This process is called metastasis. [1]

06 / HISTORY OF RESEARCH

Discovering the VHL gene

One important discovery in RCC research was the identification of the VHL gene in 1993. VHL was important for scientists to understand RCC because they could find out what helps the tumor grow. Developing new blood vessels helps RCC grow because the tumor needs oxygen and nutrients supplied by the blood. By blocking signals that help the tumor develop new blood vessels, scientists can help limit its growth. [4]

07 / WHY I BUILT THIS

My STEM fair project

Understanding how RCC cells grow and form tumors helps scientists create new treatments that could stop the growth of the tumor and eventually get rid of it. My STEM fair project is to design a website that can inform people about RCC kidney cancer and help them better understand how it develops and affects the body.

When people visit my RCC website, I hope that they will leave the website with a strong understanding of what RCC is. With that information, I want them to be interested in learning more about the cancers that take people's lives today.

03   /   TREATMENT SCIENCE

Types of treatments.

The main types of treatments for RCC are immunotherapy, targeted therapy, and surgery. One patient might need a different treatment from another depending on the stage and size of the tumor. Doctors also consider the location of the tumor and the patient's overall health. [1]

✚
01 / LOCAL TREATMENT

Surgery

Surgery treats RCC by removing the tumor. Doctors may remove only part of the kidney instead of the whole kidney because they want to preserve as much healthy kidney tissue as possible. This is called a partial nephrectomy.

Sometimes doctors need to remove the entire kidney, which is called a radical nephrectomy. Surgery is often used for early-stage RCC because the cancer may still be contained inside the kidney. [1]

⬡
02 / IMMUNE SYSTEM

Immunotherapy

Immunotherapy helps the body fight RCC by helping the patient's immune system recognize and attack cancer cells. Doctors may use it when the cancer has spread to other organs because immune cells can attack cancer cells in different parts of the body.

Immunotherapy could cause side effects because the activated immune cells could start attacking healthy cells because they mistake them for cancer cells. [2]

◎
03 / CELL SIGNALING

Targeted therapy

Targeted therapy is used to block signals that help the tumor develop new blood vessels. By reducing the tumor's supply of oxygen and nutrients, its growth can slow down.

Targeted therapy could cause side effects because blocking signals that help blood vessels grow can also affect healthy parts of the body. [3]

Why combine treatments?

When doctors combine immunotherapy and targeted therapy, it can work better than using just one treatment because targeted therapy acts as a blockade that slows down the tumor's growth. Immunotherapy acts like soldiers by helping the immune system recognize and attack cancer cells. This means that the two treatments can work together to fight cancer in different ways. [1]

Why keep researching treatments?

It is important for scientists to keep researching new treatments even though they have immunotherapy, targeted therapy, and surgery because they could discover a treatment that has fewer side effects and kills the tumor faster. Discovering new RCC treatments could improve the lives of future patients by reducing the side effects of treatments.
